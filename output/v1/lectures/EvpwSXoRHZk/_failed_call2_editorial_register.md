# Editorial Register: Layer 2 → Layer 3

*Lecture EvpwSXoRHZk (Fusion Welding, Summer 2013, session 2/6). Records the editorial judgments made in lifting the cleanup edit to the readable edition.*

## Sectioning decisions

The lecture divides into ten sections, slightly more than the 4–8 typical band, because Tom moves through many discrete teaching units (flame structure → boundary layers → acetylene history → arcs → underwater welding → flame cutting debate → flame cutting mechanics → plasma cutting → environmental regulation → liquid copper / aluminum potlines). Each section is 3–8 minutes, mapping to a clear teaching transition: a new physical phenomenon, a new piece of equipment, or a resumption from an extended anecdote.

The two trickiest boundaries:
- §4/§5: Tom segues from arc physics directly into the student's underwater-welding question. §5 is given its own section because the student question reframes the next several minutes around underwater conditions specifically. §4 ends at the conclusion of the cathode/anode fall discussion.
- §6/§7: The MAP gas perjury trial digression closes §6; §7 opens with Tom returning to the mechanics of flame cutting itself with "We do use flames for flame cutting." Tom's transition is explicit, so the section break is clean.

## Constitutional constraint (preserved verbatim per convention)

Decisions NOT permitted at layer 3:
- Adding new sentences in Tom's voice (Feynman/Leighton/Sands could because Feynman was alive to ratify; we cannot).
- Reordering content across section boundaries.
- Merging cases Tom kept separate, or splitting cases he handled as one.
- Removing factual errors Tom made on tape. (Captioner errors are corrected silently; Tom's slips are bracketed; Tom's genuine misstatements are preserved and footnoted in layer 3 only when they would actively mislead a reader.)
